## project context

This is a noncommercial **academic / school project** under deadline pressure for a browser-first isolated-sign ASL learning app with 75–100 beginner vocabulary items. Decisive constraints:

- **No pretrained CV/sign/landmark/model dependencies in the promoted lane.** This was a binding requirement in the original assignment brief; round 001 reasserted it strictly after the Stage A vestige (MediaPipe Hand Landmarker + DTW templates) was retired. See [`#arch-no-pretrained`](ARCHITECTURE.md#arch-no-pretrained).
- **Training labels and runtime dependencies are separate.** MediaPipe,
  OpenPose, YOLO, and pretrained landmark systems remain forbidden in browser
  runtime, active feature extraction, model weights, and product authority.
  Human-authored or explicitly source-approved landmark, box, mask, or region
  annotations are allowed as offline supervision targets for scratch-trained
  Detector 0/crop/landmark-target models when provenance and rights are
  recorded. Existing raw-video-only source approvals remain raw-video-only
  unless a later source-review receipt updates that exact source.
  For future hand-landmark source work only, generated labels may be considered
  as an explicitly approved offline weak-supervision fallback after
  manual/human-in-loop sources are reviewed and documented as insufficient.
  See [`#arch-no-pretrained`](ARCHITECTURE.md#arch-no-pretrained).
- **Browser-first inference** — recognition runs in the browser during normal practice; raw learner video does not upload.
- **First-party consented data** is the dataset policy; public ASL datasets are research references unless explicit rights review clears the exact use.
- **Heavy GPU training on Brev; light work (smoke/eval/export/audits) on local Mac Studio MPS.** Do not cap rows, shrink batches, sample, shorten final schedules, or choose smaller final training/evaluation datasets merely because a run would take too long locally; the user explicitly authorizes effectively unconstrained Brev/NVIDIA GPU compute through the CLI for full-scope model work. Small local runs are for smoke/proof only. See [`#arch-gpu-execution`](ARCHITECTURE.md#arch-gpu-execution).
- **Reviewer-grade evidence chain** — the 95+ hard gates in [`docs/strategy-confidence-audit.md`](docs/strategy-confidence-audit.md) are authoritative. Existing audits at `scripts/audit_*.mjs` are the validation surface; do not add a parallel system.

What this project is **not**:

- Not sentence-level ASL recognition.
- Not open-vocabulary recognition.
- Not classroom assessment.
- Not multilingual sign translation.
- Not a production-scale public deployment.

## standing checkpoint requirements

These apply to **every** checkpoint, regardless of which mission is active. The Codex executor can choose the technical path that gets them done.

1. **Keep claim boundaries explicit.**
   - What is the active claim (model id, training data, evaluation split, metrics)?
   - What scope does the claim cover (label set, signer set, recognition mode)?
   - What does it explicitly NOT claim (open-vocabulary, sentence-level, signer-disjoint at scale, etc.)?
2. **Make the claim boundary explicit.** Say whether the claim is raw RGB, keypoint, browser webcam, academic benchmark, product-domain, or learn-only. Do not let one lane inherit evidence from another.
3. **Preserve failure evidence.** If a path fails (label expansion, dataset import, model variant, ablation), record the failure with metrics and exact gate that was missed. Do not delete failed evidence; keep it as honest non-promoted artifacts.
4. **Separate labels by support status:**
   - `cv_supported_labels` — labels supported by the active passing CV claim.
   - `learn_only_labels` — promptable app labels without validated CV support.
   - `failed_cv_labels` — labels from failed or blocked CV expansions.
5. **Update repo-facing artifacts.** Validation summary, model-card metadata, status JSON, and any UI copy must agree about what is validated.
6. **Rerun or audit the relevant evidence.** Use the cheapest trustworthy check first. Do not rerun expensive experiments unless they are likely to change the checkpoint outcome.
7. **Commit significant checkpoints.** Commit after promotion / package milestones. Keep commits scoped and do not revert unrelated dirty work.
